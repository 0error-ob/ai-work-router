# AI Work Routing

## One-line strategy

Use a strong model to plan the trip skeleton (days, neighborhoods, budget breakdown), a cheap model to fill in details (restaurants, transport), a table to track costs in real time, and your own judgment to pick activities based on your actual interests.

## Recommended steps

### 1. Define your trip constraints

**What to do:**
Write down your total budget, which cities you want to visit, how many days in each, and what matters most (temples, food, nightlife, shopping).

**What to use:**
Human judgment.

**Why:**
A model can't guess whether you'd rather hike mountains or visit museums—this shapes every decision downstream.

### 2. Create a day-by-day skeleton

**What to do:**
Map out which neighborhood you'll be in each day, major landmarks or activities per day, and estimated costs per category (transport, lodging, food, activities).

**What to use:**
A strong model planning carefully.

**Why:**
This requires balancing competing constraints (time, geography, budget) that a weaker model might miss or oversimplify.

### 3. Build a cost tracker table

**What to do:**
Set up a simple spreadsheet or checklist with columns for date, category (flight, hotel, meal, activity), estimated cost, and running total.

**What to use:**
A structured-output model organizing into a table, or a spreadsheet tool.

**Why:**
Real numbers catch budget creep instantly; a model alone won't alert you if you're already 40% over budget by day 3.

### 4. Fill in restaurants, transport, and activities

**What to do:**
Find specific ramen shops, train passes, temples, museums, and walking routes that fit your skeleton and budget.

**What to use:**
A cheap/fast model executing, plus a search tool for current opening hours and prices.

**Why:**
These are low-risk, repeatable lookups; a faster model saves time and cost without sacrificing quality.

### 5. Check transport logistics

**What to do:**
Verify train connections between cities, confirm whether a JR pass or individual tickets are cheaper, check if your hotel is near a station.

**What to use:**
A tool or rule check (compare JR pass cost vs. per-journey tickets).

**Why:**
Transport is your second-biggest expense and mistakes compound across days; a calculation beats guessing.

### 6. Review the full itinerary against your budget

**What to do:**
Read through the complete 7-day plan with costs and confirm it matches your interests and stays under budget.

**What to use:**
Human judgment.

**Why:**
Only you know if a temple-heavy itinerary or a food-focused one makes you happy; a model can't make that call.

## Check points

1. **Budget math is real:** Running total in your tracker must never exceed your limit. If it does, go back to step 4 and swap activities, not hopes.
2. **Transport connections close:** Verify that your train times (especially between cities) actually exist and leave enough buffer—no 15-minute layovers.
3. **Lodging anchors the map:** Confirm your hotel is in a useful neighborhood for that day's planned activities; if not, activities shift, not the hotel.

## When to escalate

- If you find a flight or hotel deal that expires soon, decide now—don't wait for a model to help.
- If a neighborhood or activity is significantly more expensive than expected, revisit your skeleton (step 2) with a strong model rather than band-aid fixes.
- If you can't verify current opening hours or prices for a key activity online, mark it as "confirm on arrival" instead of guessing.

## When to stop automation

- **Visa requirements:** Check Japan's current visa rules yourself or with an official source; don't rely on a model for immigration rules.
- **Booking flights and hotels:** You enter payment details yourself. A model can help you narrow choices, but you finalize and pay.
- **Dietary restrictions or allergies:** If you have them, research restaurants and communicate directly; don't trust a model's assumptions about food safety.

## Final deliverable

A printable or phone-friendly 7-day itinerary with:
- Date, neighborhood, top 3–4 activities per day
- Specific restaurant or food spot names (with cost estimate)
- Transport method and time between locations
- Running cost total by day
- Backup activity if weather or energy shifts plans
